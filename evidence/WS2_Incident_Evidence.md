# OSRA Evidence Appendix B: Incident and Enforcement Evidence

**Source:** Research workstream WS2, the governance-engineering disconnect, compiled 20 March 2026.
**Published with:** OSRA v1.2.

## What this appendix is for

The architecture document cites eleven documented cases as evidence that AI operational failures originate in the substrate and pass through governance untouched. Eight are incidents and three are regulatory enforcement actions. This appendix lays each one out along the chain OSRA is built to expose: which substrate dependency was involved, how it failed, what detection existed, what the organisation was trusting without verification, and which of the three convergence conditions were present. The point is that a reader can check whether the cases actually show what they are cited for.

Citations are as recorded in the workstream. Each row states only what the cited source reports.

## What the cases show together

Three of the four patterns OSRA looks for appear repeatedly across the set, and the fourth appears in the cases where more than one condition was present.

1. The most consequential failures were silent. The Trillium drift, the Epic sepsis model, the GPU corruption and the Stanford documentation finding all describe degradation that nobody detected, because monitoring existed at the application layer and the failure was below it.
2. Trust was systematically unverified. Epic's claim went unvalidated across 170 hospitals. Presto's disclosures described a vendor's technology as its own. IBM found that most breached organisations lacked the controls they were assumed to have.
3. Compliance held while the substrate failed. Change Healthcare, the FDA cases and the DORA rationale all describe environments that had certifications or frameworks in place and still failed at the infrastructure level.
4. Where the conditions overlapped, the outcome was worst. The AWS, Epic and Change Healthcare cases each combine a substrate dependency, a detection gap and an unverified trust assumption, which is the definition of a critical convergence in Phase 4.

## What the cases do not show

They do not show that OSRA would have prevented any of them, since none of these organisations ran it. They show that the failure chain OSRA is designed to expose is the chain these incidents actually followed, and that the layer where they originated is the layer the governance in place did not audit.
